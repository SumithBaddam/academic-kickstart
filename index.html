Patents

Cross-Platform Co-Bidding with Incrementality-Based Cost Sharing

Amazon Patent Group — patent pending · 2026

I led the science behind a co-bidding system in which advertisers and Amazon jointly fund off-site ads on search and social channels, with each click's cost split in real time based on measured incrementality. Before this, Amazon carried the full cost of off-site ads, and nothing could guarantee advertiser RoAS or measure incrementality on channels that bid opaquely and report costs hours late.

  • Cost-sharing pricing model. Designed the cost-allocation mechanism that sets the advertiser's share of each click so their off-site RoAS meets its target; Amazon covers the remaining cost, so advertisers pay only for value they actually get.
  • Real-time control under delayed feedback. Developed a PID-style feedback controller with an adaptive learning rate that keeps per-click cost estimates and RoAS stable even though channel cost data is noisy and arrives hours late, preventing overspend and underspend.
  • Incrementality measurement design. Designed product-level randomized experiments with stratified sampling and a rotating holdout refreshed frequently, replacing costlier, less precise geo-based tests. The measured incrementality rate decides which clicks the advertiser is charged for.
  • Joint optimization. Built elasticity response-curve models and gradient-based optimization to find spend levels that satisfy both advertiser RoAS constraints and Amazon's net-revenue goals, setting daily spend across tiered external campaigns.
  • Bid linkage across platforms. Defined how products are assigned to off-site campaign tiers based on their on-site Sponsored Products bid percentiles and off-site performance, so external bidding responds to advertiser bids on Amazon.

Sponsored Products on Third-Party Platforms Without Real-Time Bidding

Amazon Patent Group — patent pending · 2026

I led the science for running Sponsored Products on third-party publishers that don't support real-time bidding, such as social platforms. These publishers accept only campaign-level setups, report impressions and costs in aggregate with delays, and limit how many campaigns can be created. The system keeps per-advertiser attribution, budget control, and reporting while protecting advertiser data.

  • Bidding-group design. Defined how millions of advertiser campaigns are grouped by product similarity and bid range into a small number of aggregated publisher campaigns, staying within publisher limits and hiding individual advertiser information.
  • Bid optimization. Developed the model that sets each group's bid from conversion-rate signals and target RoAS, running in a privacy-safe clean room with a third-party conversion-rate model and lookalike audiences.
  • Cost estimation at click time. Built CPC estimation from historical publisher cost data, letting the system deduct budget immediately and pause campaigns automatically before actual costs arrive.
  • Attribution and measurement. Shaped the method for attributing clicks to the right advertiser through URL reference tags, and defined how delayed, product-level impressions are mapped back to individual ads to produce unified reporting.
  • Traffic quality. Defined the signals used to filter invalid clicks out of attribution and billing.

Selected Work

Amazon Ads

Cross-platform co-bidding for Amazon advertisers

Problem. A growing share of product discovery happens on search engines and social platforms, and Amazon advertisers had no good way to reach shoppers there. When Amazon's marketing team advertises off-site, Amazon pays the whole cost while sellers get the sales. When sellers advertise alone, the reverse happens.

What I built. I led the science for a co-bidding system in which advertisers and Amazon bid jointly on external platforms and share both costs and benefits:

  • Incrementality-based cost sharing. Advertisers pay only for clicks their participation actually caused, measured with product-level randomized holdouts. This removes the incentive to shade bids or pause campaigns.
  • Real-time cost-per-click (CPC) estimation. A feedback-controlled, Bayesian-smoothed estimator gives per-click cost visibility despite publisher reporting delays, while keeping advertisers' return on ad spend (RoAS) on target.
  • Two-tier campaign optimization. It coordinates the advertiser's and Amazon's targets using elasticity data, with no extra experiments needed.
  • Large-scale validation. I ran geo experiments and product-level randomized experiments, then iterated on bidding, catalog curation, and delivery after launch.

Results.

  • On a major search platform, clicks rose 40% and net profit rose 36%. Advertisers saw a 3.3% sales lift.
  • On a major social platform, net profit rose 35–67%, and the system launched to production.
  • After launch, a series of experiments roughly doubled advertiser spend and clicks. These included campaign restructuring, catalog curation, delivery-score tuning, and a landing-page fix that cut mismatched landings by about 85%.

Amazon Ads

Visual ad sourcing for an image-first platform

Problem. On a visual discovery platform, users browse by image, but ad sourcing relied only on keywords.

What I built. I took over the project mid-flight and shipped a production pipeline. It processes about 10M images a day, builds multimodal (image and text) embeddings, matches them against about 20M product embeddings using approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search, and publishes the results to a cache used for real-time ad serving. I also merged two separate workflows into one automated system.

Results.

  • Daily runtime fell 43% (21 hours to 12 hours) and compute cost fell about 60%.
  • Two scientists no longer had to monitor and trigger jobs by hand.
  • Serving integration caused no regressions.

Amazon Alexa

Speech recognition error correction for voice shopping

Problem. Voice assistants often misrecognize product and brand names, especially new products, so customers end up with the wrong item in their cart.

What I built.

  • Benchmarking. I compared the in-house speech recognition (ASR) model against Whisper. I generated synthetic test voices from speaker embeddings (a BiLSTM model) and a text-to-speech model, Bark.
  • Error correction. I built an error-aware sequence-to-sequence model (T5 and a shopping-pretrained variant) that uses conversation context, browsing history, and cart items to correct and rephrase item names. It was trained on 5.2M examples, including synthetic errors from an ASR error simulator.
  • Deployment. I worked with engineers to put the model into the production contextual speech service.

Results.

  • The error correction rate reached 41% (versus 34% for the baseline).
  • Word error rate fell 16% on rephrased queries.
  • The model correctly left 99.2% of already-correct utterances alone.
  • A/B testing showed about 100K improved customer interactions per day.
